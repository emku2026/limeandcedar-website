How car shipping works

You tell us where the car is, where it's going and when it's ready. We give you a real price — one that carriers on that route actually accept — and a pickup window. When you're ready, you sign the booking online. Nothing is charged at that point.

Your dispatcher then posts the load to licensed carriers, checks the one who takes it (active FMCSA authority and cargo insurance) and sends you the driver's details. Only then do you get the deposit invoice. The balance is paid to the driver at delivery.

Open carrier: what it means

Most cars in the U.S. travel on open two-level trailers carrying 7–10 vehicles. It's safe, it's how new cars reach dealerships, and because the truck is shared, it's the cheapest option. Your car is exposed to the weather like it would be on the road. For classic, exotic or freshly painted cars, consider enclosed transport.

What we ship

  • Sedans, coupes, hatchbacks and EVs (Tesla included)
  • SUVs and minivans
  • Pickups, including lifted and dually trucks (please mention modifications)
  • Inoperable vehicles — carriers with a winch, for a small extra charge
  • Motorcycles

How to prepare your car

  • Leave about ¼ tank of fuel — not more.
  • Remove personal items and toll tags; carriers aren't insured for contents.
  • Take your own photos the day before pickup.
  • Note any leaks, low clearance or alarms and tell your dispatcher.

Car shipping FAQ

How much does it cost to ship a car?

On an open trailer, regional moves are often $300–$700, half the country $700–$1,100 and coast to coast $1,000–$1,600. The exact price depends on the route, vehicle size, season and whether it runs. Send the form for a real number.

How fast can you pick up my car?

Most cars are picked up within 1–5 days of booking. Busy interstate routes are quicker; rural pickups can take longer.

Do I need to be there at pickup and delivery?

You or someone you trust (18+) needs to hand over the keys and sign the inspection report at both ends.

Is door-to-door really to my door?

Yes, as close as a large truck can legally and safely get. On narrow streets or in gated communities the driver may ask to meet at a nearby open lot.
