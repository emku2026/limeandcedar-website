Car shipping to and from Florida

Why Florida routes behave differently

Florida sits at the far end of the map: almost every truck that comes in has to drive back out the same way. That makes the state very seasonal. In fall, carriers fill up on the way south and can come back north half empty; in spring it flips. Knowing which direction is busy helps you pick a date and get picked up sooner.

Most traffic runs through four metro areas — Miami–Fort Lauderdale, Orlando, Tampa and Jacksonville. Carriers come in on I-95 along the Atlantic coast (from the Northeast), I-75 down the middle of the state (from the Midwest, Atlanta and Tampa), and I-10 across the Panhandle (from Texas, the South and California). Inside the state, I-4 links Tampa and Orlando, and Florida's Turnpike runs from Central Florida down to Miami. Pickups along these corridors are quickest; the Keys, the Panhandle's small towns and rural inland areas can take a few extra days.

Snowbird season

Every year thousands of people from New York, New England, Ohio, Michigan and Canada spend the winter in Florida and ship their car instead of driving it. The rush south runs from October through January; the rush back north runs from March through May. During those months trucks on I-95 and I-75 fill early, so book 2–3 weeks ahead and give a flexible pickup window if you can.

Shipping a second car or a convertible you'd rather keep out of the weather? Enclosed transport keeps it covered the whole way. Many snowbirds also send a bike down for the season — see motorcycle shipping.

Weather and seasons

  • Hurricane season (June–November) is the big one. When a storm is forecast, carriers stop loading in the affected area and some roads close or turn into evacuation routes. Your car waits safely until it passes; pickup or delivery moves by a few days.
  • After a major storm demand jumps — flood-damaged cars head to auctions, people replace vehicles — and trucks get busy for a few weeks. Plan a little extra time.
  • Summer rain and heat: afternoon thunderstorms are normal and rarely slow a carrier. Leave about ¼ tank of fuel and make sure the battery is healthy if the car will sit at an auction yard.

Auction cars and equipment from Florida

Florida has many Copart and IAA yards — around Miami, Fort Lauderdale, Orlando, Tampa, Jacksonville and the Panhandle — and a lot of storm-damaged and salvage vehicles pass through them. Send us the lot number, the yard address and the pickup deadline; yards start charging storage after a few free days. If the car doesn't run or roll, we book a carrier with a winch.

We also arrange transport for tractors, excavators and other heavy equipment on flatbed, step deck and RGN trailers, with permits for oversize loads.

Moving to Florida?

Florida asks new residents to title and register an out-of-state vehicle within 10 days of starting a job, enrolling children in a Florida public school, or otherwise establishing residency. A vehicle coming from another state also needs a VIN verification, which a tax collector's office, a law enforcement officer or a notary can usually do. Requirements change, so check the current rules on the Florida Highway Safety and Motor Vehicles website before your car arrives.

Florida car shipping FAQ

How long does it take to ship a car from New York to Florida?

New York to Miami is about 1,280 miles — usually 3 days on the road, plus 1–5 days to get your car picked up after booking. In snowbird season, booking 2–3 weeks ahead helps.

When is the best time to ship a car to Florida?

Late summer and early fall, before the October–January snowbird rush, are usually easiest. If you must ship in peak season, book early and keep your pickup dates flexible.

What happens if a hurricane hits while my car is in transit?

The carrier waits until roads are safe and authorities allow travel. We keep you updated on the new pickup or delivery date.

Can you pick up a car from a Copart or IAA yard in Florida?

Yes. Send us the lot number, yard address and any pickup deadline. Non-running cars are loaded by carriers with a winch.

Do I pay anything before a carrier is assigned?

No. You get the deposit invoice only after a carrier is assigned to your vehicle. The balance is paid to the driver at delivery.
