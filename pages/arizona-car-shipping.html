How Arizona car shipping works on the map

Most Arizona shipments start or end in the Phoenix area — Phoenix, Mesa, Scottsdale, Chandler, Gilbert, Tempe and the West Valley. Tucson is the second big market, two hours south. Flagstaff, Prescott and Yuma are smaller, but carriers serve them regularly because they sit right on the main interstates.

Carriers cross the state on four roads. I-10 is the busiest: Los Angeles ↔ Phoenix ↔ Tucson ↔ El Paso and the rest of Texas. I-17 climbs from Phoenix up to Flagstaff, where it meets I-40, the east–west route across northern Arizona toward New Mexico, Oklahoma and Southern California. I-8 runs from Casa Grande through Yuma to San Diego. Pickups along I-10 in Phoenix and Tucson are the fastest; small towns far from the interstates, the reservations in the northeast and remote ranch addresses can take a few extra days.

California → Arizona is one of the busiest lanes in the West

Every year many Californians move to Phoenix, Tucson and the suburbs around them, and a lot of trucks run that lane in both directions. Los Angeles to Phoenix is a one-day drive for a carrier, and the Bay Area to Phoenix usually takes two. Because the lane is so busy, it's often one of the easier routes to book. We're based in the San Francisco Bay Area and ship it every week.

Snowbird season

Arizona is a major winter destination. People from the Midwest, the Pacific Northwest and the states along the Canadian border — Minnesota, Wisconsin, Michigan, the Dakotas, Washington and others — come to Phoenix, Scottsdale, Mesa and Tucson for the winter and ship their car instead of driving it. Cars come in from October through January and go back home from March through May. In those months trucks fill early, so book 2–3 weeks ahead and give a flexible pickup window if you can.

Bringing a classic or a car you'd rather keep out of the dust? Enclosed transport keeps it covered the whole way. Many winter visitors also send a bike down for the riding season — see motorcycle shipping.

Weather and seasons

  • Monsoon season (July–September) brings sudden thunderstorms and dust storms (haboobs), mostly around Phoenix, Tucson and along I-10. Visibility can drop to almost nothing, and drivers pull over until it passes. These stops are usually short — an hour or a few hours — but they can push a delivery back a little.
  • Summer heat in Phoenix and Yuma often passes 110°F. It's hard on weak batteries: make sure the battery is healthy and leave about ¼ tank of fuel, especially if the car will sit at an auction yard.
  • Winter snow up north: Flagstaff sits at about 7,000 feet, and snow can close I-17 and I-40 for a few hours or a day. Pickups and deliveries in Flagstaff and Prescott may move by a day in a storm; the deserts around Phoenix and Tucson stay clear.

Auction cars and equipment from Arizona

Copart and IAA have yards around Phoenix and Tucson, and the dry climate means many auction cars there have little rust — a reason buyers from other states bid on them. Send us the lot number, the yard address and the pickup deadline; yards start charging storage after a few free days. If the car doesn't run or roll, we book a carrier with a winch.

We also arrange transport for tractors, excavators and other heavy equipment for construction, mining and farm work in Arizona, on flatbed, step deck and RGN trailers with permits for oversize loads.

Moving to Arizona?

New residents need to register their vehicle with the Arizona Motor Vehicle Division (MVD). Cars registered in the Phoenix and Tucson areas generally also need to pass an emissions test first. Requirements change, so check the current steps on the Arizona MVD website before your car arrives.

Arizona car shipping FAQ

How long does it take to ship a car from California to Arizona?

Los Angeles to Phoenix is about 370 miles — usually 1 day on the road. The Bay Area to Phoenix is about 750 miles, usually 2 days. Add 1–5 days to get your car picked up after booking.

When should I book for snowbird season?

Book 2–3 weeks ahead if your car is coming to Arizona between October and January or going home between March and May. Flexible pickup dates help a lot in those months.

Do dust storms or snow delay deliveries?

Sometimes, briefly. Drivers stop for haboobs in monsoon season and for snow closures on I-17 and I-40 near Flagstaff in winter. We keep you updated if a date moves.

Can you pick up a car from a Copart or IAA yard in Arizona?

Yes. Send us the lot number, yard address and any pickup deadline. Non-running cars are loaded by carriers with a winch.

Do I pay anything before a carrier is assigned?

No. You get the deposit invoice only after a carrier is assigned to your vehicle. The balance is paid to the driver at delivery.
