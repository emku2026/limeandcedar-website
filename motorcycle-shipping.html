How we ship motorcycles

Motorcycles travel on open or enclosed trailers, secured upright with soft straps and wheel chocks — never laid down. Many carriers who move cars also carry bikes, which keeps the price down on popular routes. For high-value or vintage bikes we recommend an enclosed trailer.

Bikes we move

  • Sport bikes and naked bikes
  • Cruisers and baggers (Harley-Davidson, Indian and others)
  • Touring bikes and trikes
  • Dirt bikes and dual-sports
  • Vintage and project bikes, including non-running

Getting your bike ready

  • Fuel tank no more than ¼ full.
  • Remove saddlebags, GPS units, loose covers and anything that can come off.
  • Check the battery is charged and the tires are inflated.
  • Tell us about leaks, alarms and the key situation.
  • Take photos from every side the day before pickup.

Rally and seasonal shipping

Planning a ride at Daytona Bike Week or Sturgis, or wintering in Florida or Arizona? Book early — trucks heading to big events fill up fast. Ship one way and ride the other, or ship both ways.

Motorcycle shipping FAQ

How much does it cost to ship a motorcycle?

Usually less than a car on the same route, because a bike takes less space. The price depends on distance, the route, open or enclosed trailer and the season. Send the form for a real number.

Do I need to crate my motorcycle?

No. We ship bikes uncrated, strapped upright on the trailer. Crating is only needed for some international or freight shipments.

Can you ship a motorcycle that doesn't run?

Yes. Let us know when you request a quote so we send a carrier who can load it safely.

Can I ship a motorcycle with a car?

Yes — ship both on the same truck and save. Mention both vehicles in the form.
